    MYST

    Type with your eyes. Select with a wink.

    Multi-Sensory Yielding Support Technology is a virtual keyboard for people with paralysis and motor disabilities. It needs only a standard webcam: no keyboard, mouse, touchscreen or special hardware.

    Team
    Niranjan ER, Samina Anna Sajin, Desna Shaji and me
    Context
    Internship, Christ College
    View on GitHub
    MYSTTracking gaze
    Highlight jumps: 0.0/s

    Gaze navigation

    Horizontal gaze slides a highlight across the keys. Normalized iris tracking, EMA smoothing, a 7-frame majority vote and gaze locking keep it steady.

    Wink to select

    A deliberate wink picks the key. MYST tells it from a natural blink by comparing the blendshape scores of both eyes.

    Help while typing

    Bigram word prediction on the Brown corpus, autocorrect and text-to-speech, across QWERTY and symbol layouts.

    What didn’t work

    Before splitting navigation from selection, we tried pure gaze selection six ways. None held up on a webcam in real use.

    • Sclera detection
    • Iris-to-eyelid ratios
    • Eyelid distance
    • Vertical blendshapes
    • Real-time calibrationSmall errors added up until the cursor drifted.
    • Full 2D gaze estimationFine in controlled light, not in real use.

    After 13+ versions: gaze navigates, a wink selects.

    ARIA

    Pinch the air to switch a fan and a bulb.

    ARIA reads your hand through a webcam, draws a glass interface you point at, and sends commands over WiFi to an ESP32 that switches two relays. No touch, no remote.

    Stack
    Python, MediaPipe, ESP32
    Hardware
    2-channel relay, fan, bulb
    ARIA
    Index fingertip: hover
    • Click a device to send a command.

    Index fingertip

    Your fingertip is the cursor. Hover over a device to target it.

    Thumb and index pinch

    A click. Pinch over a device to switch it on or off.

    Index and middle pinch

    Grab a device and drag it anywhere on the screen.

    How it’s wired

    • LaptopWebcam, MediaPipe hand tracking, OpenCV interface
    • WiFiTCP connection to the ESP32 on port 8080
    • ESP32GPIO 23 to relay IN1, GPIO 22 to relay IN2
    • RelaysIN1 switches the fan, IN2 switches the bulb

    Protocol

    DeviceLaptop sendsESP32 replies
    FanLED:ON LED:OFFSTATE:LED:…
    BulbRELAY:ON RELAY:OFFSTATE:RELAY:…

    The wire names stay LED and RELAY to match the firmware. A mock ESP32 script listens on the same port, so the whole loop runs on one laptop.
